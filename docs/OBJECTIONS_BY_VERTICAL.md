## How to use

The CLOSING_PLAYBOOK has the universal objections (price, timing, "send me info", "let me think"). This doc covers **vertical-specific** objections — the ones rooted in their industry's quirks.

For each objection: (1) what they're really saying, (2) word-for-word response, (3) reframe.

## HVAC

### "We're slammed right now, call me in [season]"
**What they're really saying:** "I have more work than I can handle, why would I add more?"

**Response:**
> "That's exactly why we should talk now — not when you're swamped. Right now you're probably turning down jobs because of capacity. Imagine if you could route those overflow jobs to a wait list instead of losing them. That's what we'd build before peak season hits. Got 5 min Wednesday for a quick walk-through?"

**Reframe:** busy season = highest stakes for missed calls.

---

### "We have a marketing guy / agency"
**What they're really saying:** "I've been burned, don't want another vendor."

**Response:**
> "Cool — we don't replace marketing agencies. We capture the calls THEIR ads are generating that you're losing because no one's answering. We're the catcher's mitt. Most of our clients keep their existing agency."

**Reframe:** complementary, not competitive.

---

### "We tried [other AI tool] and it sucked"
**What they're really saying:** AI sales tools have burned them before.

**Response:**
> "Yeah, most of them are generic — built for Silicon Valley software companies and shoved into HVAC. Mine is built specifically for [HVAC]. I'll show you the call recordings of how it handles a 'my AC is leaking water' call vs how [other tool] handles it. Want me to send the side-by-side?"

**Reframe:** specificity > generality.

---

### "My techs handle the calls"
**What they're really saying:** "My existing process is fine."

**Response:**
> "When your techs are in someone's attic, can they answer? When they're driving 65 on the freeway? Our system covers the moments they CAN'T. They keep doing the work. We capture the calls they miss."

**Reframe:** AI fills coverage gaps, doesn't replace people.

---

### "I don't want to lose the personal touch"
**What they're really saying:** Customer service identity.

**Response:**
> "Totally with you — and that's why we don't replace you. The AI handles the first contact (book the time, get the address) and YOU show up in person to the actual job. Your customers love you because of what you do at their house, not because of who answers the phone at 2 AM."

**Reframe:** AI handles intake, human handles service.

## Dental

### "We're HIPAA compliant — your tool isn't"
**What they're really saying:** "Liability and compliance terrify me."

**Response:**
> "We've built specifically for HIPAA — BAA on file, encrypted data, no PHI stored on third-party servers. I'll send you our compliance one-pager and our BAA template tonight. We can run a 30-day pilot with NO patient data exposure if you want extra comfort."

**Reframe:** specifically built for healthcare.

**Implementation note:** for dental especially, you MUST have a Business Associate Agreement (BAA) ready. Use Twilio HIPAA-eligible tier ($150/mo+), not standard. Or Bland AI which has a HIPAA tier.

---

### "Our front desk knows our patients"
**What they're really saying:** Personal relationships matter.

**Response:**
> "Yeah, and your front desk is the best part of your practice. We're not replacing them — we're freeing them. Right now they spend 40% of their day on hold with insurance, leaving voicemails, confirming appointments. AI handles those. Your front desk gets to actually talk to your patients when they walk in."

**Reframe:** AI does the boring work, humans do the relationship work.

---

### "Insurance billing is too complex for AI"
**What they're really saying:** "AI can't navigate my actual workflow."

**Response:**
> "Agreed — we don't touch billing. What we DO is verify benefits at the front-end before the patient walks in, so your billing person isn't surprised. Saves them 10-15 hours a week. Want me to walk you through it?"

**Reframe:** front-end verification, not billing replacement.

---

### "Our patients are older, they don't like AI"
**What they're really saying:** Demographic concern.

**Response:**
> "The voice agent we use sounds completely human — 90% of patients don't know it's AI. The ones who do find out usually say they prefer it because it's available at 9 PM. The ones who hate AI? They get routed to your front desk during business hours like normal."

**Reframe:** indistinguishable from human, with human fallback.

## Plumbing

### "I'm a one-truck operation"
**What they're really saying:** "I'm too small for this."

**Response:**
> "Perfect — that's actually our sweet spot. Solo plumbers miss the most calls because you're literally underground or under a sink. Adding 5 booked jobs a month at $400 average is $2,000 in new revenue for $750/mo in cost. You'd net $1,250+. Math works at every size."

**Reframe:** solo operators benefit MOST from AI coverage.

---

### "Most of my work is emergency, can AI handle that?"
**What they're really saying:** "My customers are stressed, AI will fail."

**Response:**
> "Specifically built for that. The AI's first move on emergencies is: confirm severity (water actively flooding? gas smell? sewer backup?), get your address, route severe ones DIRECTLY to your phone with a text alert. Lower-severity ones get scheduled. You don't miss the real emergencies."

**Reframe:** triage, not gatekeeping.

---

### "I get most jobs through word-of-mouth"
**What they're really saying:** "I don't need outbound."

**Response:**
> "Word-of-mouth is gold — but here's what AI does: it asks every happy customer for a Google review at the right moment (right after the job, while they're still grateful). 90 days from now, you have 40+ new 5-star reviews. That fuels MORE word-of-mouth and more Google traffic. We're amplifying what's already working."

**Reframe:** AI amplifies word-of-mouth via review velocity.

## Med Spa / Cosmetic

### "Our brand is high-touch luxury, AI feels cheap"
**What they're really saying:** Brand image concern.

**Response:**
> "I hear you. We've built specifically for high-end practices. The voice agent uses a premium voice (think Apple Siri quality), introduces itself as 'concierge,' and is configured to match YOUR brand voice. We can send a sample call to your number right now if you want to hear it. If it doesn't sound luxury, we don't deploy."

**Reframe:** premium AI for premium brands; demo before deploy.

---

### "Our procedures need consultation, AI can't do that"
**What they're really saying:** "Real conversion requires human."

**Response:**
> "Right — we don't replace your consultation. We BOOK the consultation. Right now, when someone fills out a form at midnight, you respond Tuesday. That lead is gone. Our AI calls them back in 60 seconds, qualifies them, books them. They show up to YOUR consultation."

**Reframe:** intake, not sales.

---

### "Our front desk is part of the experience"
**What they're really saying:** Receptionist role is sacred.

**Response:**
> "We don't touch the in-person experience. We handle inquiries that come in OUTSIDE business hours, which is when 60% of luxury cosmetic searches happen. Your front desk owns the in-person relationship. AI owns the 8 PM web visitor."

**Reframe:** off-hours coverage.

## Roofing / Solar

### "I'm tired of lead-gen scams"
**What they're really saying:** Burned by storm-chasers, lead aggregators.

**Response:**
> "Same. We're not a lead-gen company — we don't sell you leads. We capture the leads YOU already generate via Google, your truck, your yard signs. Those leads call your phone and most of them ring out. We answer them. You own every lead, no per-lead fees."

**Reframe:** capture YOUR leads, not sell new ones.

---

### "Storm chasers ruin our reputation, AI feels similar"
**What they're really saying:** "AI = scammy."

**Response:**
> "Totally fair. The AI doesn't make outbound cold calls. It only handles INBOUND — people calling YOU. So your brand stays untouched. Want me to send you a recording of how we handle a 'roof leak after storm' call vs a typical answering service?"

**Reframe:** inbound only; brand-safe.

---

### "Insurance work is most of our revenue, AI can't handle that"
**What they're really saying:** "My workflow is too custom."

**Response:**
> "Agreed — we don't touch insurance claim processing. We DO help homeowners understand they can get a free inspection covered by insurance, and book them into your schedule. Insurance work, just better-funneled."

**Reframe:** funnel for insurance work, not handler.

## Chiropractic / Wellness

### "Our patients want personal care"
**What they're really saying:** Identity-as-personal-care.

**Response:**
> "100%. The AI does ZERO clinical work. What it DOES is: confirm your appointments (cuts no-shows by 60%), follow up on patients who missed visit 5, check in on patients post-adjustment. The patient feels cared for at scale. Your clinical time is preserved."

**Reframe:** care beyond the appointment, not in it.

---

### "We're cash-pay, conversion is hard, AI can't sell"
**What they're really saying:** "Conversion takes a real human."

**Response:**
> "AI doesn't close memberships — it WARMS them up. It calls patients between visit 6 and 7 and gently asks how they're feeling, then mentions the membership program. By visit 8, they're primed when YOU pitch. We see 35% lift in membership conversion."

**Reframe:** warm-up, not closer.

---

### "I'm a small practice, this is overkill"
**What they're really saying:** Cost concern dressed as scale concern.

**Response:**
> "Smaller practices benefit MORE per dollar. You don't have a marketing director, a front-desk team, or a follow-up specialist. AI is all of those for $750/mo. A 1-doctor practice that adds 8 patient visits a month at $80 each = $640. We're net-neutral by month 2 and pure profit after."

**Reframe:** small practices = highest ROI per dollar.

## Universal vertical-agnostic objections (refresher)

These appear in CLOSING_PLAYBOOK but deserve the vertical-specific lens here too.

### "Send me information"
**Universal:** "Sure — what's your email? While I have you, one quick question…"
**Vertical-twist:** "I'll send you a 5-min video. Plus, I know you're [HVAC/dental/etc.] — I can send you a case study from a [same vertical] shop similar to yours. Want both?"

---

### "We're not interested"
**Universal:** "Understood. Last question — is it the AI angle, the price, or the timing?"
**Vertical-twist:** "Got it. Out of curiosity — when you guys [hire / build / scale next], what would the trigger be? I'll just keep my notes for then."

---

### "I need to talk to my [partner / wife / accountant]"
**Universal:** "Totally fair. What would they need to see to greenlight this?"
**Vertical-twist (HVAC partner):** "Yeah, partnerships need alignment. Here's an idea — let me send a 90-second Loom they can watch on their phone tonight. Calls are easier to skip than a 90-second video. Cool?"

## The "no" recovery framework

Across ALL verticals, when you get a hard no, run this 3-step:

1. **Acknowledge fully:** "Totally fair, no problem."
2. **Re-anchor:** "Out of curiosity, when [a similar event] happens [next year / when you scale / during peak season], what's your plan?"
3. **Plant seed:** "Cool — I'll keep an eye out. Last thing: cool if I send you a 30-day case study now and again in October when [season] hits?"

This converts ~10-15% of hard no's into Q3/Q4 books.

## What to NEVER say

❌ "I understand" (unless followed by something specific)
❌ "I get it" (lazy)
❌ "Have you tried..." (defensive)
❌ "Most of my clients..." (without specifics)
❌ "Just give me a chance" (begs)
❌ "What would it take..." (used way too often, reads like a sales movie)
❌ "Trust me" (instantly suspicious)

---

## When to walk away

Some prospects are unsellable. Don't chase. Walk if:

- They've said no 3+ times in one call
- They start asking about technical details to derail (rabbit-hole tactic)
- They ask you to "send a proposal first" before any sales call
- They require multi-stakeholder consensus before talking
- They say "we have a guy" and shut down all reframes

Take the no, log them as "Q3 follow-up", move to next dial. Volume is your strategy.
